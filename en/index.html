Traditional Thai massage · Nuad Bo-Rarn

Authentic traditional Thai massage: no oil, light clothing, on the mat. Trained at the International Training Massage School in Chiang Mai, with three years of practice in the country.

Traditional Thai massage, or Nuad Bo-Rarn, is done on a floor mat, fully clothed and without oil. It combines palm and thumb pressure, assisted stretches and work along the energy lines, the sen. It is not the oil-based relaxing massage often sold under the same name in spas.

I represent no Thai massage school. I studied with several teachers and at a few schools, and each left something behind. What I offer is the practice I absorbed over three years living in Thailand, under my own responsibility.

The Thai session, moment by moment

The traditional sequence begins at the feet and rises. There is a reason for it: the body accepts touch from the ground upwards more readily. Touch a number to see each moment.

  1. 1. Conversation — A few minutes on sleep, pain and surgeries.
  2. 2. Wai khru — The silent opening gesture, in respect to the teachers. It marks where the session's time begins.
  3. 3. Feet and legs — Face up, with palm and thumb pressure along the inner and outer lines.
  4. 4. Hips and abdomen — Hip openings and light work on the belly, always following the breath.
  5. 5. Back — Face down, pressure using body weight along the spine, without impact or abrupt manoeuvres.
  6. 6. Arms and hands — Forearm, wrist and each finger, an area nearly always forgotten by people who type all day.
  7. 7. Neck and head — Seated or lying down, with gentle traction and points at the base of the skull.
  8. 8. Stretches and rest — Assisted stretches come at the end, once the tissue has already yielded. Then a few still minutes.

Questions about the classes and the sessions

What is Traditional Indian Yoga?
It is the Sadhana practised at Shivam Yoga, under the guidance of Mestre Arnaldo, in the Samkhya and Tantra view, with techniques for awakening awareness and concentration, pujas as a transmission of energy and gratitude, breathing and detoxification exercises, strengthening work, relaxation techniques and mantras.
Do I need experience for a Shivam Yoga class?
No. The class lasts one hour and requires no previous experience. It is a dive into self-knowledge and into the tantric culture of Shivam Yoga. Each posture has versions, from the simplest to the most demanding.
What is traditional Thai massage?
It is Nuad Bo-Rarn, practised in Thailand for centuries. You stay clothed, lying on a floor mat, and receive palm and thumb pressure, assisted stretches and work on the sen lines. No oil and no table.
How is it different from a relaxing massage?
A relaxing massage glides over the skin with oil. Thai massage works deeply with body weight, on points and lines, and includes stretches you could not do on your own, close to some asanas in terms of position. Thai massage has its roots in India. It is a good way to feel lighter, ease pain and reduce stress, among other benefits.
Do I need to undress?
No. The session is done clothed, in light comfortable clothing, without oil.
Does it hurt?
The pressure is firm, from the gentlest to the most demanding, for a better result. Say at any point if you want it lighter, and I adjust straight away. Above all, self-observation is present throughout.
How long does a session last?
A full Nuad Bo-Rarn session lasts one hour. The seated chair version, without oil, lasts fifteen minutes.
How do I know it is authentic?
By its form: a floor mat, light clothing, no oil, a sequence that starts at the feet and rises, and assisted stretches at the end. If it happens on a table, with oil and no stretching, it is something else under the same name.
Can I learn Thai massage?
Yes. I teach the fundamentals to people who already practise and want to refine their technique, and to complete beginners.
